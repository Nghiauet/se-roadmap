<Lead>
You cannot fix what you cannot see. Observability is the discipline of leaving enough
breadcrumbs in production that you can answer questions you didn't anticipate. Three
signals — logs, metrics, traces — each answer a different question.
</Lead>

## The big idea

The three pillars are not redundant; they're complementary.

## Logs — but structured

Plain-text logs are useless at scale. Use **structured logging**: every log line is a
JSON object with named fields.

<Compare>
  <Do>
    ```json
    { "ts": "2025-11-12T08:00:00Z", "level": "info",
      "msg": "order.placed", "order_id": 9001, "user_id": 42,
      "request_id": "req_abc", "latency_ms": 87 }
    ```
    Searchable, aggregatable, machine-parsable.
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    ```text
    08:00:00 INFO Order 9001 placed by user 42 in 87ms (req_abc)
    ```
    Each parser regex is its own bug.
  </Dont>
</Compare>

Threads to weave through every log:

<DefinitionList>
  <Definition term="request_id / trace_id">Stamp every request at the gateway; propagate via headers to every downstream call.</Definition>
  <Definition term="user_id / tenant_id">Filter all logs for one customer's tickets.</Definition>
  <Definition term="service / version">Find the bad deploy.</Definition>
  <Definition term="level">`debug` / `info` / `warn` / `error` — never log `info` 10× per request.</Definition>
</DefinitionList>

## Metrics

A metric is a *number you can graph*. Three flavours:

<DefinitionList>
  <Definition term="Counter">Monotonic — `http_requests_total`. Take the rate over time.</Definition>
  <Definition term="Gauge">Up and down — `db_connections_active`, `queue_depth`.</Definition>
  <Definition term="Histogram / summary">Distribution of values — `request_latency_seconds`. The basis of percentiles.</Definition>
</DefinitionList>

The **RED method** for any service:

## Tracing

A trace stitches together every span (DB call, RPC, internal function) for one request
across services.

```text
trace_id=abc123
├─ HTTP GET /orders                   [api-gateway]   142ms
│  ├─ auth.verify                      [auth-svc]      9ms
│  ├─ orders.list                      [orders-svc]   118ms
│  │  ├─ db.query (SELECT … FROM orders)              92ms  ← the slow span
│  │  └─ cache.lookup                                   2ms
│  └─ encode_response                                   3ms
```

Two minutes of staring at a trace replaces an hour of guessing. Use **OpenTelemetry** as
the API; most backends (Jaeger, Tempo, Honeycomb, Datadog) speak it.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Sample intelligently">
Tracing every request costs serious money at scale. Sample 1% of healthy traffic,
**always** trace errors and slow requests. Most systems support tail-based sampling for
this.
</Callout>

## Alerting

You'll have hundreds of metrics; alert on a handful.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Alert on symptoms, not causes">
    "User-facing latency p99 > 2s" beats "CPU > 80% on db-3."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Page only on what wakes you usefully">
    A page that you ignore is worse than no page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pair every page with a runbook">
    The first thing the on-call sees should be "if this fired, do X."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track alert noise">
    A flapping alert that fires nightly trains people to ignore real alerts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## In practice

Start small: structured logs with request IDs, RED metrics on every service, traces on
critical paths. Promote one engineer per quarter to **observability owner** — somebody
who watches dashboards, removes noisy alerts, and adds the field everyone needs but
nobody added. The pillar is staff time as much as tooling.
